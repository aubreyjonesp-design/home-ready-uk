Where the water figures come from

GOV.UK Prepare says there is no standard figure, because emergencies vary and people use different amounts. It says the World Health Organization recommends at least 2.5 to 3 litres of drinking water per person per day for survival, and that 10 litres per person per day is more comfortable, as it also covers basic cooking and washing. You may need extra water for baby formula, medical devices and pets.[1]

We use 3 litres a day for the minimum. For babies we count a person’s share and remind you to keep ready-to-feed formula. Pet amounts are a rough guide only (about 1 litre a day per dog, 0.25 litres per cat or small pet); ask your vet if unsure. GOV.UK suggests buying sealed bottled water rather than filling your own containers.[2]

Build it up over time. Buy the food and drink you normally use, and eat the oldest first, so nothing is wasted.

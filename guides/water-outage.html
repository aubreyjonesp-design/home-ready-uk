No water: what to do

Last checked: 15 October 2026

Most water cuts are fixed quickly. Longer cuts are rare, but they do happen, so it is worth a little planning. Your water company is the best place for news.

How likely is a long water cut?

The UK government’s National Risk Register 2026 looks at a rare “reasonable worst case”, not an everyday water cut. Its worst case for “Water infrastructure failure or loss of drinking water” is likelihood 4 (5–25%) and impact 5 (Catastrophic). Likelihood here means the chance of this happening at least once in 5 years.

In the government’s words, it imagines “the loss of piped drinking water and impact to wastewater services for over one million people”. It says “residents would likely have to leave the area, and some may require evacuation”, and that “repair of the infrastructure could take months”.

The register also says “all risks in the NSRA are relatively low likelihood events”. You do not need to store months of water. The steps below help with a short cut, and help you cope well if you are ever asked to leave.

Get ready now

  • Keep some sealed bottled water. GOV.UK says at least 2.5 to 3 litres per person per day to drink, and 10 litres per person per day to be more comfortable. Use our calculator to work out your amount. GOV.UK says “there is no standard figure” for how many days to store, because emergencies vary.
  • Buy sealed bottled water rather than filling your own containers. GOV.UK says bottled water is sealed in a food-grade bottle, so it stays safe for longer.
  • Store it well and use it in turn. Bristol Water advises keeping bottled water in a cool, shaded place away from direct sunlight. Do not keep it somewhere warm, like a car: the water can taste odd or turn green, and then you should not drink it. Check the date on the bottles, use the oldest first, and replace them as you go.
  • Find out who your water company is. Use Water UK: find your supplier, then follow it on social media or sign up for its text updates.
  • Join your water company’s Priority Services Register if you or someone you care for needs extra help. Water companies use it to deliver bottled water to people who can’t collect it. See our Priority Services Register guide.
  • If someone uses medical equipment that needs water (for example home dialysis), GOV.UK says to talk to your care team about a plan for a water cut.
  • Keep handy supplies. CCW, the water consumer body, suggests hand sanitiser, wet wipes, waterless soap, dry shampoo and disposable plates, cups and cutlery.
  • If you have space, a water butt gives you water for flushing the toilet. Do not drink it (see our rainwater guide).
  • Know where your stop tap is, so you can turn the water off if a pipe leaks.
  • Keep a bag ready in case you need to leave, with medicines and important documents (see our household plan).

During a water cut

  • Check your water company’s website, text updates or social media. Ofwat says your company should tell you where to get another supply and when it expects water to be back.
  • Save bottled water for drinking, cooking, washing hands and making baby formula. If you cannot boil water, GOV.UK suggests ready-to-use baby formula.
  • Check on neighbours who may struggle to collect water.

If it lasts longer

Bottled water stations

  • Ofwat says that if the piped supply fails, water companies must provide at least 10 litres of water per person per day within the first 24 hours, and keep this up until the supply is back. This can be bottled water, a water tanker (bowser) or a standpipe.
  • Companies often set up bottled water stations. Your water company’s website and social media will say where they are and when they are open.
  • If you are on the Priority Services Register, your water company should arrange water for you. If it doesn’t arrive, contact them. If you are not registered but need help, CCW says to contact your water company anyway. Help may be slower than if you were already registered.
  • Staff and delivery drivers should carry ID. If you are unsure, CCW says to ask them to wait outside while you call the number on your water bill.

Toilets without flushing

  • While the water is off, your toilet will not refill after you flush it.
  • South West Water and Bristol Water advise flushing with water from a water butt or your stored water: fill a bucket and pour it into the bowl.
  • Do not flush wet wipes. South West Water says to put them in the bin.

Keeping clean

  • To wash your hands, GOV.UK says to fill a large clean bowl, like a washing-up bowl, with water and add soap or household disinfectant.
  • Hand sanitiser, wet wipes and dry shampoo help you stay clean and save water.
  • Use disposable plates and cutlery so you don’t need to wash up.
  • Soft and fizzy drinks from the cupboard are safe to drink. GOV.UK says not to drink milk or fresh fruit juice that smells off.

If you are asked to leave

If the authorities ask you to leave, go. The National Risk Register says: “It is important for people not to delay evacuating properties, buildings or general locality if asked to by the responding authorities.”

  • Follow instructions from your water company, the council and the emergency services. Check for Emergency Alerts on your phone.
  • Take your bag, with medicines and important documents, and any pets.
  • Before you go, turn off your water at the stop tap.

When the water comes back

  • It may look cloudy or discoloured. GOV.UK says to run the cold tap nearest your stop tap (often under the kitchen sink) for a while. If it doesn’t clear, contact your water company.
  • You may be entitled to compensation. CCW says your water company must consider this after an incident.

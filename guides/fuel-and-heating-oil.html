Fuel shortages and heating oil

Last checked: 15 October 2026

Fuel shortages are uncommon and usually short. Homes heated by oil, and people who rely on a car in rural areas, can prepare with a few simple habits.

What the National Risk Register says

The register’s worst case for “Insolvency affecting fuel supply” (likelihood 4, 5–25%; impact 1, Minor) is a fuel company failing, which “could cause regional disruption to the production and supply of refined fuels, impacting road transport, aviation and domestic heating fuel.” It says “Impacts would be greatest during winter months”, and that sudden “adaptive purchasing” (buying more than usual) “could result in temporary fuel shortages.”

The National Risk Register 2026 describes rare, national “reasonable worst cases”. In its own words, “all risks in the NSRA are relatively low likelihood events.” Likelihood is the chance of the worst case happening at least once in 5 years (2 years for malicious risks).

Get ready now

Do these now, while things are calm. You can do them bit by bit.

  • Check your heating oil level regularly, and don’t wait until it is nearly empty. Citizens Advice says keep at least a quarter of a tank while you wait for a delivery, or the system could be damaged.
  • Order early. Citizens Advice says oil is usually cheaper in summer and early autumn, and a delivery in a few weeks is usually cheaper than an urgent one.
  • Shop around. Citizens Advice suggests getting at least 3 quotes from members of UKIFDA, the fuel distributors’ trade body.
  • Think about an oil club, where neighbours order together. Your local council may know of one (Citizens Advice).
  • Aged 75 or over? Citizens Advice says your supplier should add you to the Cold Weather Priority scheme, but check you are on it with each supplier.
  • Check your tank for cracks, leaks or rust, keep the area around it clear for deliveries, and get your boiler serviced each year by an OFTEC-registered technician (UKIFDA).
  • Have a back-up for warmth: warm layers, blankets and hot water bottles. See our cold weather guide.

If there is a fuel shortage

  • Buy as you normally would. The register says buying more than usual can turn a small problem into a temporary shortage.
  • The government’s fuel plan allows limits on how much fuel you can buy per visit, and priority fuel for emergency services, so everyone can get some.
  • Never use a barbecue, camping stove or generator indoors for heat. See our fire and carbon monoxide guide.

Afterwards

  • Top up as normal, rather than filling up in a rush.
  • If you are struggling with heating costs, Citizens Advice lists help for people who use oil, LPG, coal or wood.

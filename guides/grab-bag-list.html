Emergency grab bag list (UK)

Last checked: 18 October 2026

A grab bag is a spare bag with the things you would need if you were asked to leave home quickly, for example because of flooding. GOV.UK Prepare suggests keeping one ready. Here is a simple list to start from.

If there is a fire, get out and stay out. Do not stop to take anything, including your grab bag (GOV.UK Prepare). Call 999.

What to pack

This list is based on the British Red Cross “emergency kit on the move” and GOV.UK Prepare. Keep the bag small enough to carry.

Essentials
☐Your regular medicines for a few days, and a written list of what you take and the doses.
☐Paper instructions and charged back-up batteries for any medical equipment (GOV.UK Prepare).
☐Your phone and charger, or a charged power bank.
☐A paper list of important numbers, in case your phone dies.
☐Cash and bank cards.
☐Spare glasses or contact lenses.
☐Clothes for the weather, including something warm and waterproof.
☐Bottled water and ready-to-eat snacks. A flask for a hot drink if you have time.
Papers
☐Copies of important documents, such as passports, birth certificates and insurance details, in a waterproof bag (British Red Cross, GOV.UK Prepare).
☐A copy of your household emergency plan.
For children, babies and pets
☐Nappies, baby food or ready-to-feed formula, and a favourite toy for each child (GOV.UK personal flood plan).
☐Pet food, any pet medicines and a carrier. GOV.UK says many evacuation centres will let you bring your pet.
Extra for flooding
☐A torch and spare batteries (GOV.UK personal flood plan).
☐Wellington boots and rubber gloves (Natural Resources Wales).
☐Your insurance company’s 24-hour number and your policy number, and Floodline: 0345 988 1188 (Natural Resources Wales).

Where to keep it

  • Keep it near the door or with your home emergency kit, and tell everyone at home where it is.
  • Check it twice a year: phone chargers, dates on food and medicines, and clothes that still fit the children.
  • Your home emergency kit is for staying in. The grab bag is for leaving. Some things, like a torch, can live in either.

If you are asked to leave

  • Go when you are told to. Take your grab bag, medicines and pets.
  • Turn off your gas, electricity and water if it is safe and you have time. GOV.UK Prepare says you will need to do this if you leave your home, so find out now where the switches and stop tap are.
  • Follow advice from the emergency services, your council and local radio.
  • Let family or friends know you are safe.

When cards and banking stop: cash and staying safe online

Last checked: 15 October 2026

Card payments, banking apps and cash machines very rarely stop working, but when they do it can be for days. A little cash at home and good online security help.

What the National Risk Register says

The register’s worst case for “Technological failure at a systemically important retail bank” (likelihood 3, 1–5%; impact 3, Moderate) is critical technology out “for at least 2 days”, with customers “unable to view account balances, process payments, use online banking or withdraw cash from ATMs.” It says “The most significant impact would be felt by vulnerable customers with only a single bank account.”

The National Risk Register 2026 describes rare, national “reasonable worst cases”. In its own words, “all risks in the NSRA are relatively low likelihood events.” Likelihood is the chance of the worst case happening at least once in 5 years (2 years for malicious risks).

Get ready now

Do these now, while things are calm. You can do them bit by bit.

  • Keep a small amount of cash at home. West Yorkshire Prepared says to keep some “in case cash points and electronic payment systems stop working.” The British Red Cross suggests cash for essentials like food or fuel. Small notes and coins are easiest.
  • Secure your email account first, then your other accounts. GOV.UK Prepare says to use a passkey where you can, or a strong, unique password with 2-step verification.
  • Use a password manager, or your browser’s, to create and store strong passwords (GOV.UK Prepare).
  • Turn on automatic updates for your phone, computer and smart devices (GOV.UK Prepare).
  • Back up photos and files, and know how to restore them (GOV.UK Prepare).
  • Save these numbers: 159 to reach your bank safely, Report Fraud on 0300 123 2040 (Police Scotland: 101).

If payments stop working

  • Use your cash for essentials like food, medicine and fuel.
  • Follow updates from your bank and the news.
  • Watch out for scams. The register says a bank outage would bring “heightened fraud”. Ready Scotland warns criminals may take advantage of emergencies.
  • Check on anyone who may struggle, such as older neighbours who rely on one account.

Spotting and reporting scams

  • An unexpected call about money? Hang up and call 159. Stop Scams UK says 159 connects you safely to your bank, covers more than 99% of UK current accounts, and cannot be spoofed.
  • Forward suspicious emails to report@phishing.gov.uk and suspicious texts to 7726 (NCSC). Don’t click links in them.
  • If you have lost money or been hacked, report it to Report Fraud or call 0300 123 2040 (England, Wales and Northern Ireland). In Scotland, call Police Scotland on 101. Tell your bank straight away.

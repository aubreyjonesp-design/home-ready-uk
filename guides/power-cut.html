Power cut: what to do

Last checked: 15 October 2026

Most power cuts in the UK are short and local. A little planning makes them much easier.

Report a power cut: call 105 (free, England, Scotland and Wales). In Northern Ireland call 03457 643643. If someone is in danger, call 999.

Get ready now

  • Keep a torch (battery or wind-up) and spare batteries where you can find them in the dark. Torches are safer than candles.
  • Keep a charged power bank for your phone, and a battery or wind-up radio.
  • Write 105 and other key numbers on paper. Your phone or internet may not work. Use our one-page plan.
  • Keep some bottled water and food that does not need cooking, plus a tin opener.
  • Remember: gas boilers, heat pumps and home internet usually need power too.
  • If you rely on a landline or a personal alarm (telecare), ask your provider if it works in a power cut. They may offer a back-up battery.
  • If you or someone you care for could need extra help, join the Priority Services Register.
  • Find your “load block” letter at powercut105.com. It would only matter in the unlikely case of planned rota power cuts.

During a power cut

  • Check if neighbours have power too. If only your home is off, check your fuse box (trip switches).
  • Call 105 or check powercut105.com for updates.
  • Switch off things that could be dangerous when power comes back, such as cookers and heaters. Leave one light on so you know when it is back.
  • Keep the fridge and freezer closed to keep food cold for longer. See the Food Standards Agency advice on food safety in a power cut.
  • Wrap up warm. Check on older neighbours or anyone who lives alone.
  • Never use a barbecue, camping stove or generator indoors. They can give off carbon monoxide.

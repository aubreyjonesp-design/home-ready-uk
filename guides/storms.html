Storms and high winds

Last checked: 15 October 2026

Storms hit the UK most years, mainly in autumn and winter. Simple steps before a storm keep you safer and cut the damage.

What the National Risk Register says

The register rates Storms at likelihood 4 (5–25%) and impact 3 (Moderate). Its worst case is “storm force winds affecting multiple regions of the UK”, with “gusts in excess of 85 mph”. It says disruption to power, communications and transport “could last for 1‑4 days and for more than 5 days in remote rural locations.”

The National Risk Register 2026 describes rare, national “reasonable worst cases”. In its own words, “all risks in the NSRA are relatively low likelihood events.” Likelihood is the chance of the worst case happening at least once in 5 years (2 years for malicious risks).

Get ready now

Do these now, while things are calm. You can do them bit by bit.

  • Get weather warnings. Check the Met Office warnings or use the Met Office app, where you can set alerts for your area. GOV.UK Prepare says to pay extra attention when a storm is named.
  • Check your roof, chimney and gutters. The British Red Cross suggests checking for loose tiles, aerials and satellite dishes, having a professional check chimney stacks, and clearing gutters.
  • Check fences, gates and trees. Make sure fences and gates are in good condition, and deal with loose or overhanging branches (Red Cross).
  • Know who your power provider is and that you can call 105 in a power cut. See our power cut guide.
  • Have a torch, radio and power bank ready, and a paper list of numbers.

When a storm is forecast

  • Secure or bring in loose things: ladders, trampolines, garden furniture, toys and bins (GOV.UK Prepare).
  • Close and fasten doors and windows, especially on the side facing the wind, and garage doors. Close and bolt the loft hatch.
  • Park cars in a garage, or away from buildings, trees, walls and fences.
  • If chimney stacks are tall or in poor condition, move beds away from the area directly below them.
  • Charge phones, power banks and back-up batteries for medical equipment.
  • Check whether schools are closing, and limit travel. Follow any ‘Do Not Travel’ advice.
  • Check on anyone who might need help.

During the storm

  • Stay inside if you can. Keep away from windows. Open inside doors only as needed, and close them behind you.
  • If you must go out, don’t walk or shelter near buildings, walls, fences or trees. Use the door on the sheltered side.
  • Do not go out to repair damage while the storm is still going.
  • Keep well away from fallen power lines. GOV.UK Prepare says high-voltage electricity can jump gaps with no warning.
  • If you must drive, take care on bridges and high, open roads, and watch for side winds.

After the storm

  • Don’t touch cables that have been blown down or are still hanging.
  • Don’t walk too close to walls, buildings and trees, as they may have been weakened.
  • Check on vulnerable neighbours and relatives and help them arrange repairs.
  • Storms can also cause flooding and water cuts. See our flooding and no water guides.

If you’re suddenly in hospital: a back-up plan for carers

Last checked: 15 October 2026

If you look after someone, or have children or pets who rely on you, think now about who would step in if you were suddenly ill or had an accident.

Get ready now

Do these now, while things are calm. You can do them bit by bit.

  • Choose a back-up person, such as a relative, friend or neighbour, who could step in for a few hours while proper arrangements are made (NHS).
  • Make sure they can get in. The NHS says they should have door keys or know the code to a key safe, and know what care is needed.
  • Write care notes and leave them somewhere obvious. Carers UK suggests including: the person’s name and address, who to contact, their medicines and where they are kept, allergies, GP and pharmacy, any treatment, care services, continence products, mobility aids, and anything others need to know about behaviour.
  • Share the plan. Talk it through with the person you care for and your emergency contacts, and give them a copy or tell them where it is (Carers UK). Review it regularly.
  • Ask for a carer’s assessment. The NHS says to contact adult social services at your local council. It can cover someone taking over so you can have a break.
  • Find the council’s out-of-hours team. Carers UK says councils have an emergency duty team you can contact when the office is closed. In Northern Ireland, use your Health and Social Care Trust.
  • Make your information easy to find. If appropriate, GOV.UK Prepare suggests wearing medical alert tags or bracelets and adding medical information to your phone.
  • Include pets and children in the plan: who would feed and walk pets, or collect children from school.
  • Use the Carers UK planning guide to build your plan, and add your back-up person to our household plan.

If it happens

  • Tell hospital staff straight away that you are a carer and someone is relying on you. The NHS says you’ll be asked for an emergency contact when you are admitted.
  • Call your back-up person, or ask someone to call them for you.
  • If no one can help, call the council’s adult social services or emergency duty team. If anyone is at immediate risk, call 999 (Carers UK).
  • For health advice for yourself or the person you look after, call NHS 111.

Afterwards

  • Update your plan with anything you learned.
  • Talk to the council about extra support, such as respite care, if you need it.
  • If you need to talk, Carers UK points to Samaritans, free on 116 123, at any time.

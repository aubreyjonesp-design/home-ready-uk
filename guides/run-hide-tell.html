Terrorism: Run, Hide, Tell

Last checked: 15 October 2026

The chance of being caught up in a terrorist attack is low. Knowing three simple words could help keep you and others safe.

What the National Risk Register says

The register rates “Terrorist attacks in publicly accessible locations” at likelihood 5 (more than 25%) and impact 4 (Significant). For malicious risks, likelihood is measured over 2 years. GOV.UK Prepare says: “the chance of being caught up in a terrorist incident remains low”.

The National Risk Register 2026 describes rare, national “reasonable worst cases”. In its own words, “all risks in the NSRA are relatively low likelihood events.” Likelihood is the chance of the worst case happening at least once in 5 years (2 years for malicious risks).

RUN to a place of safety. HIDE if you can’t run: silence your phone and turn off vibrate. TELL the police by calling 999 when it is safe.

Get ready now

Do these now, while things are calm. You can do them bit by bit.

  • Learn Run, Hide, Tell and talk about it with your family.
  • Notice where the exits are when you are in buildings and on public transport (British Red Cross).
  • Save the Anti-Terrorism Hotline: 0800 789 321. You can also report online at ACT. In an emergency, always call 999.
  • Learn some first aid. See our first aid guide.
  • Travelling abroad? GOV.UK Prepare says check the Foreign Office travel advice for the country first.

If you are caught up in an attack

  • Run if there is a safe route. Insist others come with you, but don’t let them slow you down. Leave your belongings. Don’t film the incident.
  • Hide if you can’t run. Find cover from gunfire behind solid walls. Stay quiet, silence your phone and turn off vibrate. Lock or barricade yourself in if you can, and stay away from the door.
  • Tell the police on 999 when it is safe. Say what is happening, where, where the attackers are, and describe them. If you can’t speak, listen to the call taker’s instructions.

Afterwards

  • If you saw anything useful, tell the police.
  • You may be in shock without realising it. See a doctor (British Red Cross).
  • The government-funded Support Hub for victims and survivors of terrorism has a free 24-hour line: 0808 168 9111.

Leaving home quickly, or staying inside safely

Last checked: 15 October 2026

Sometimes the safest thing is to leave your home. Other times you will be told to go indoors and stay there. A little planning makes both much easier.

What the National Risk Register says

Evacuation comes up across many risks. For example, the register’s worst case for loss of water says “residents would likely have to leave the area”. It advises: “It is important for people not to delay evacuating properties, buildings or general locality if asked to by the responding authorities.”

It also says people may be asked to “‘go in, stay in and tune in’ to local radio stations or check official sources of information online”.

The National Risk Register 2026 describes rare, national “reasonable worst cases”. In its own words, “all risks in the NSRA are relatively low likelihood events.” Likelihood is the chance of the worst case happening at least once in 5 years (2 years for malicious risks).

Get ready now

Do these now, while things are calm. You can do them bit by bit.

  • Pack a grab bag. GOV.UK Prepare suggests keeping things you would need if asked to leave quickly in a spare bag. The British Red Cross suggests: medicines, a phone charger or power bank, cash and bank cards, a paper list of contact numbers, spare glasses, weather-appropriate clothing, water and snacks, and baby and pet supplies.
  • Keep documents together. GOV.UK Prepare suggests keeping your plan with passports, birth certificates and insurance papers, so they are easy to gather if you are asked to evacuate. The Red Cross suggests copies in a waterproof bag.
  • Keep enough medicine for several days, plus paper instructions and charged back-up batteries for any medical equipment (GOV.UK Prepare).
  • Know how to turn off your gas, electricity and water. GOV.UK Prepare says you will need to do this if you have to leave your home. Write where they are on your household plan.
  • Agree a meeting place close to home, in case you can’t get back or phones are down. Ready Scotland says it should have shelter and be accessible for everyone in your household.
  • Plan for anyone who can’t leave on their own. Ready Scotland says you may need extra planning if someone has a disability, a long-term condition or uses medical equipment. See GOV.UK Prepare’s advice for disabled people and carers.
  • Get pets ready. The RSPCA suggests suitable carriers, a supply of food, and a watertight bag with microchip numbers, vet and vaccination details and a photo of your pet. GOV.UK’s flood plan says many evacuation centres will let you bring your pet.
  • Make sure you can get Emergency Alerts on your mobile phone, and keep a battery or wind-up radio (see our phones and internet guide).

If you are asked to leave

  • Go when you are asked. Do not wait to see what happens. The register says delaying “may put individuals’ own lives at risk, as well as putting emergency responders in danger”.
  • Take your grab bag, medicines and pets. Turn off gas, electricity and water if it is safe and you have time.
  • Follow official advice on where to go: the council, emergency services, local radio and Emergency Alerts.
  • If your home is on fire, get out and take nothing. GOV.UK Prepare says you should not take anything with you. See our fire guide.

If you are told to go in, stay in, tune in

This is the usual advice for a chemical, smoke or radiation incident nearby.

  • Go in. UKHSA says to go into any building as soon as possible (not the one where the incident is). A car does not protect you well enough.
  • Stay in. Close doors and windows. Turn off fans and air conditioning, and close vents, including trickle vents in windows, if you can (UKHSA).
  • Tune in. Follow local radio, TV, official websites and social media, and Emergency Alerts. You may need to stay in for some hours. For a radiation emergency, GOV.UK Prepare says it could be one to two days.
  • If you need your regular medicine, UKHSA says to contact NHS 111 for advice rather than leaving the building.
  • If you think a chemical has got on you, GOV.UK Prepare says: remove yourself from the area, remove outer clothing (try not to pull it over your head), and remove the substance from your skin with a dry absorbent material.

Afterwards

  • Only go home when the authorities say it is safe.
  • Restock your grab bag and replace any medicines you used.

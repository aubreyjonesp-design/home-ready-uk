Staying in touch when phones and the internet are down

Last checked: 15 October 2026

Phone and internet outages are usually short and local. But storms and power cuts can sometimes take them out for days, so it helps to have a back-up plan.

What the National Risk Register says

The register’s worst case for “Simultaneous loss of all fixed and mobile forms of communication” (likelihood 3, 1–5%; impact 3, Moderate) imagines one region losing all connections, “with most fixed‑line connections remaining offline for several days due to a lack of power at the customer premises and damage to overhead cables.”

New risks in 2026 include “Digital resilience failure” (likelihood 4, 5–25%; impact 4, Significant), such as “a faulty software update that crashes critical servers”, based on the 2024 CrowdStrike outage.

Severe space weather (likelihood 4, 5–25%; impact 4, Significant) could last “for 1‑2 weeks”, with “regional power disruptions, loss or disruption of Global Navigation Satellite Systems (for example, Global Positioning System (GPS)) and some telecommunications”. The steps on this page and in our power cut guide help with all of these.

The National Risk Register 2026 describes rare, national “reasonable worst cases”. In its own words, “all risks in the NSRA are relatively low likelihood events.” Likelihood is the chance of the worst case happening at least once in 5 years (2 years for malicious risks).

Get ready now

Do these now, while things are calm. You can do them bit by bit.

  • Keep a battery or wind-up FM radio and spare batteries. GOV.UK Prepare says updates may be given on the radio during an outage.
  • Write important numbers on paper: family, neighbours, GP, utilities. Use our household plan.
  • Agree a meeting point with family in case you can’t reach each other. Choose somewhere easy to find and open to the public.
  • Keep a power bank charged for each phone.
  • Still on an old analogue landline? GOV.UK Prepare says the analogue network is being upgraded to digital and to contact your landline provider. West Yorkshire Prepared says the switchover is expected to finish by January 2027. Digital landlines need power to work.
  • Telecare and personal alarm users: call your landline provider. Digital Phone Switchover says the change could stop telecare alarms working. GOV.UK Prepare says if you are a vulnerable customer, such as a telecare user, your provider must give you a free back-up battery unit, so your landline works for at least an hour in a power cut.
  • Tell your provider if you need extra support. GOV.UK Prepare says this can mean priority fault repair and extra help during outages.
  • Check how medical devices behave offline. Ask the maker how a personal alarm or diabetes sensor would work if mobile or broadband is lost.
  • Download or print maps. GOV.UK Prepare says GPS may still work with no signal, but map apps need maps downloaded in advance.
  • Agree a back-up plan with anyone vulnerable, for example that you or a neighbour will visit in person if you can’t call.

During an outage

  • You can still call 999 from a mobile. GOV.UK Prepare says emergency calls automatically roam onto any available network, and the 999 system is designed to keep running in power cuts.
  • If your mobile network is down but your Wi-Fi works, try Wi-Fi Calling.
  • Use your phone sparingly to save battery. Messages may work when calls don’t. Moving a short distance can help, and public Wi-Fi may still work.
  • Check your provider’s status page or app. Ofcom says to report a fault to your provider if it isn’t listed, and tell them if you rely on the connection for telecare or health devices.
  • Check on neighbours, especially anyone who lives alone.

Afterwards

  • Recharge power banks and replace radio batteries.
  • Ofcom says you may be entitled to compensation for a loss of service. Ask your provider.

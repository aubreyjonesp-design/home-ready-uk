Fire and carbon monoxide safety at home

Last checked: 15 October 2026

Most home fires start by accident, and carbon monoxide can’t be seen or smelt. Working alarms and a simple escape plan protect everyone.

If there is a fire: get out, stay out, call 999. Do not try to tackle it yourself. If you think a gas appliance is leaking carbon monoxide, call 0800 111 999 (free, 24 hours) and go outside.

Get ready now

Do these now, while things are calm. You can do them bit by bit.

  • Fit smoke alarms on every floor and test them at least once a month. GOV.UK Prepare says you are 11 times more likely to die in a fire if you have no working smoke alarms. In Scotland, the law on heat and smoke alarms changed in 2022.
  • Fit a carbon monoxide alarm that makes a loud sound. The NHS says to have one in each room with an appliance that burns gas, oil, coal or wood.
  • Get appliances serviced. The NHS says to have boilers, heaters and cookers serviced regularly by a qualified, registered engineer, and to keep chimneys and flues clean.
  • Plan and practise your escape route so everyone knows what to do. Keep exits clear and keep door and window keys where everyone can find them (GOV.UK Prepare).
  • Do a free home fire safety check. Use the online home fire safety check or contact your local fire service. In Northern Ireland, ask NIFRS for a free Home Fire Safety Check.
  • Charge safely. Follow the maker’s instructions for phones, laptops, e-bikes and e-scooters, use a compatible charger, and don’t charge them on your escape route (GOV.UK Prepare).
  • Keep heaters clear of curtains and furniture, and never use them to dry clothes. Use torches rather than candles in a power cut.
  • Teach children what the smoke alarm sounds like and how to “stop, drop and roll” (GOV.UK Prepare).

A quick bedtime check

GOV.UK Prepare says you are more at risk from fire while asleep. Before bed:

  • close inside doors
  • turn off and unplug appliances unless they are meant to stay on, like the freezer
  • check the cooker is off, and don’t leave the washing machine or dryer running
  • put candles and cigarettes out properly

If there is a fire

  • Get everyone out as soon as possible. Do not assume everyone has heard the alarm. Some children may not wake to it.
  • Don’t stop to investigate or pick up valuables. If there is smoke, keep low where the air is clearer.
  • Check doors before opening them. If a door is warm, don’t open it. Close doors behind you as you leave.
  • Call 999 once you are out, and alert neighbours if they are close by.
  • If you can’t get out: get everyone into one room, ideally with a window and a phone. Put bedding around the bottom of the door. Call 999, then open the window and shout “HELP FIRE”.
  • If clothes catch fire: stop, drop and roll, and smother the flames with a heavy coat or blanket.

Carbon monoxide

The NHS says symptoms include a headache, dizziness, feeling sick, feeling weak, tiredness and confusion, chest or muscle pain, and shortness of breath. They may get better when you go outside. The HSE says warning signs include yellow or orange flames instead of blue, soot or staining around appliances, and pilot lights that keep going out.

  • If you think there is carbon monoxide, stop using the appliance, go outside, and get medical advice. Don’t go back in until you have advice (NHS).
  • Call NHS 111 if you think you have symptoms. Call 999 if someone is struggling to breathe, suddenly confused, unconscious, weak, or has chest or muscle pain. Do not drive yourself to A&E.
  • Never use a barbecue or camping stove indoors, and never run engines in an enclosed space like a garage (NHS).

After a fire

  • The fire service will tell you whether it is safe to go back in, and give you an incident number. The British Red Cross says you will need it for insurance.
  • Call your insurer as soon as you can. If you rent, tell your landlord. If you can’t stay at home and aren’t insured, contact your local council.
  • Check with a pharmacist before using any medicine that was exposed to heat.

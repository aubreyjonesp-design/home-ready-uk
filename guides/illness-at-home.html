Illness at home: flu, norovirus and pandemics

Last checked: 15 October 2026

Winter bugs are common, and most people get better at home. The same simple habits also help if a bigger outbreak or pandemic ever happens.

What the National Risk Register says

The register rates Pandemic at likelihood 4 (5–25%) and impact 5 (Catastrophic), one of its highest-scoring risks. Its worst case is “an unmitigated pandemic”, with a response stage “at least 9 months and potentially, significantly longer”. An outbreak of an emerging infectious disease is rated likelihood 4, impact 4 (Significant).

The National Risk Register 2026 describes rare, national “reasonable worst cases”. In its own words, “all risks in the NSRA are relatively low likelihood events.” Likelihood is the chance of the worst case happening at least once in 5 years (2 years for malicious risks).

Get ready now

Do these now, while things are calm. You can do them bit by bit.

  • Keep up to date with vaccines, including the flu vaccine if you are offered it. GOV.UK Prepare says vaccinations are the most effective way to prevent many infectious diseases.
  • Keep a small sick-day kit: paracetamol or ibuprofen, a thermometer, tissues, soap, and oral rehydration sachets (the NHS says a pharmacist may recommend these for diarrhoea and vomiting).
  • Keep enough of your regular medicines to last several days (GOV.UK Prepare).
  • Keep some easy food and drinks in the cupboard. See our food storage guide.
  • Agree who could help with shopping, medicines or pets if your household is ill.
  • Take any unused antibiotics back to a pharmacy. GOV.UK Prepare says don’t save them for later or share them.

Stop it spreading

  • Wash your hands with soap and water for at least 20 seconds, especially after the toilet and before preparing food. Hand gel does not kill norovirus (NHS).
  • Cover coughs and sneezes with a tissue, then bin it. Clean things people touch a lot, like door handles, taps and flush handles.
  • Let fresh air in.
  • If you are unwell, try to stay at home and avoid contact with others. With norovirus, the NHS says stay off school or work until you have not been sick or had diarrhoea for 2 days, and don’t visit hospitals or care homes.
  • Wash clothes and bedding with vomit or poo on them separately, on a 60°C wash.

Looking after yourself at home

  • Flu: rest and sleep, drink plenty of water, and take paracetamol or ibuprofen if needed. Don’t give aspirin to under-16s. Antibiotics do not work for flu.
  • Norovirus: drink lots of fluids, in small sips if you feel sick. Avoid fruit juice and fizzy drinks. It usually gets better in 2 to 3 days.
  • Get help from NHS 111 or your GP if you are worried about a baby or child, are pregnant, have a long-term condition or weak immune system, or flu symptoms don’t improve after 7 days.
  • Call 999 if someone has severe difficulty breathing or starts coughing up blood.

In a bigger outbreak

  • Follow official advice from GOV.UK and the NHS. Advice will depend on the illness.
  • Check on neighbours and relatives by phone, and offer to drop off shopping or medicine.
  • Shop normally. Our food storage guide helps you build a small store over time, so there’s no need to stock up in a hurry.

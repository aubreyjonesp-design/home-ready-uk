Flooding: before, during and after

Last checked: 15 October 2026

Flooding can happen from rivers, the sea, heavy rain and groundwater, sometimes far from a river. A little planning reduces the damage and the worry.

If you are in danger, call 999. Never walk or drive through flood water. GOV.UK Prepare says driving through flood water is the number one cause of death in floods.

Get ready now

Do these now, while things are calm. Each one only takes a few minutes or a small cost.

  • Check your flood risk using the links below for your nation.
  • Sign up for free flood warnings (England, Scotland, Wales), online or by calling Floodline on 0345 988 1188.
  • Write your flood plan: who to call, where you would go, and how to turn off gas, electricity and water. Use the GOV.UK personal flood plan or our household plan.
  • Pack a grab bag: medicines, first aid kit, torch, bottled water, warm waterproof clothes, baby items and copies of documents.
  • Put documents in sealed waterproof bags and keep valuables upstairs or up high (GOV.UK Prepare).
  • Check your home insurance covers flooding, and write the policy number and helpline on your plan.
  • Save key numbers, such as your council, insurer and utilities (nidirect suggests a list of useful contacts).
  • Tell neighbours, especially older or vulnerable people, and join the Priority Services Register if you need extra help.

Check your risk and get free warnings

  • England: the Environment Agency. Check for flooding, check your long-term risk and sign up for free flood warnings.
  • Scotland: SEPA. SEPA flooding and sign up to Floodline.
  • Wales: Natural Resources Wales (NRW). Check flood warnings and sign up for warnings.
  • Northern Ireland: there is no Floodline warning service. GOV.UK says to use flood maps to check your risk. See Department for Infrastructure: Rivers and flooding and nidirect: Flooding. To report flooding in Northern Ireland, call the Flooding Incident Line on 0300 2000 100 (24 hours).

Floodline: 0345 988 1188 (24 hours; textphone 0345 602 6340) for England, Scotland and Wales. GOV.UK lists 0345 numbers as UK-wide numbers that are charged, not free. You can sign up for warnings by phone. If you have a disability or need extra help, you can ask Floodline to send warnings to a friend or relative too. Warnings do not cover surface water (flash) flooding.

The warning levels are: Flood alert (be prepared), Flood warning (take action now) and Severe flood warning (danger to life).

Make a flood plan

  • Use the GOV.UK personal flood plan, or the flood section of our household plan.
  • Know how to turn off your gas, electricity and water.
  • Agree where you would go, and how you will contact each other. Plan for pets and medicines.
  • Pack a grab bag: medicines, a first aid kit, important documents in sealed waterproof bags, a torch, bottled water, warm waterproof clothes and baby items.
  • Keep valuable and sentimental things upstairs or up high.
  • If your risk is high, look at ways to protect your property, such as flood barriers or air brick covers.
  • Let neighbours know about the risk, especially older or vulnerable people, and join the Priority Services Register if you need extra help.

When a flood is coming or happening

  • Turn off gas, electricity and water if it is safe. Do not touch electrical switches or plugs if you are standing in water.
  • Move people, pets, cars and important things to safety, for example upstairs or to higher ground.
  • Put flood protection in place if you have it. Block sinks, baths and toilets, and unplug washing machines and dishwashers.
  • If the emergency services tell you to leave, leave. Delaying puts you and rescuers at risk.
  • Stay in a high place with a way out if you cannot leave.

After a flood

  • Only go back when the emergency services say it is safe.
  • Do not turn on gas, electricity or water until your supplier or a qualified engineer has checked them. If you smell gas, call 0800 111 999 (Great Britain).
  • Take photos and video of the damage and mark the water level on the wall. Talk to your insurer before you throw things away or start repairs.
  • Do not use tap water if it changes colour, taste or smell. Contact your water company. Do not eat food that has touched flood water.
  • GOV.UK says to throw away fridge food if the power was off for more than 4 hours, and freezer food if it was off for more than 24 hours.
  • If you rent, your landlord is responsible for repairs. If you are not insured or cannot live at home, contact your local council.

Health risks of flood water

The UK Health Security Agency (UKHSA) says flood water can contain sewage and pollutants, and can hide sharp objects and open manholes. Its advice includes:

  • Wear rubber boots, waterproof gloves and an apron when cleaning up. Cover cuts with waterproof plasters.
  • Wash your hands well with warm soapy water after touching flood water or anything it has touched. Use bottled water if your water supply is affected.
  • Clean hard surfaces with hot water and detergent. Clean and disinfect kitchen items before use, and throw away wooden boards and utensils that touched flood water. Wash fabrics at 60°C.
  • Never use petrol or diesel generators indoors, and keep heaters and dryers well ventilated, because of carbon monoxide.
  • Get gas or oil heating checked by a qualified engineer before you use it.
  • Feeling tired, anxious or unable to sleep is normal after a flood. Talk to your GP, call NHS 111, or contact the Samaritans.

Insurance basics

  • Check your home insurance covers flood damage, and keep the policy number on your plan.
  • Flood Re is a joint scheme between the government and insurers that helps make flood cover more affordable for homes at high risk. You buy insurance in the usual way. Your insurer can pass the flood part of the policy to Flood Re. Use the Flood Re tool to check if your home qualifies. Prices and terms are set by insurers.
  • The Association of British Insurers has advice on flood claims.

Cold weather: staying warm and well

Last checked: 15 October 2026

Cold weather can affect anyone’s health, and some people are at higher risk. A few simple steps help keep you, your home and your neighbours safe.

Hypothermia is a medical emergency. If you think someone has it, call 999 or go to A&E. See our first aid guide.

Warnings and alerts

  • Met Office weather warnings (yellow, amber, red) cover snow, ice, wind and rain across the UK. Red means dangerous weather is expected and you should act now. See Met Office UK warnings.
  • Cold-Health Alerts come from the UK Health Security Agency (UKHSA) with the Met Office. They warn when cold is likely to affect health, mainly between 1 November and 31 March. They are aimed at health and care services but anyone can view them or sign up. See the Weather-Health Alerting System and current alerts.

Who is most at risk

UKHSA lists people aged 65 and over, people with long-term health conditions, pregnant women, young children (especially aged 5 and under), people with learning disabilities, people at risk of falls, people who live alone, are housebound or have low mobility, and people in deprived circumstances.

Keeping warm (UKHSA advice)

  • Try to heat rooms you spend a lot of time in, such as the living room or bedroom, to at least 18°C.
  • Reduce draughts, for example with draught excluders. Keep bedroom windows closed at night.
  • Wear several thin layers rather than one thick one.
  • Try not to sit still for more than an hour or so. Stretching your arms and legs helps.
  • Wear shoes with good grip when it is icy.
  • If bad weather is forecast, check you have enough medicine and food. Find someone who could fetch them if you cannot go out.
  • Get your flu and COVID-19 vaccines if you are eligible (see NHS winter health).
  • Have heating and cooking appliances checked by a Gas Safe registered engineer, and fit a carbon monoxide alarm. See our no gas or heating guide.

Frozen and burst pipes

From the Met Office:

  • Know where your stop tap is and check it works. It is often under the kitchen sink.
  • If a pipe is frozen, turn off the stop tap. Thaw it slowly with hot water bottles or towels soaked in hot water. Never use a naked flame or blowtorch.
  • If a pipe bursts, turn off the stop tap and the boiler, open taps to drain the system (save some water in a bucket for flushing and hand washing), and soak up water with towels.
  • If water has leaked near electrics, do not touch them. Switch them off at the fuse box.
  • Use a WaterSafe approved plumber for repairs.

Check on neighbours

UKHSA says older neighbours, family and friends may need extra support in cold weather. Keep in touch, ask if they feel unwell or need practical help, and see if they need help stocking up on food and medicine if bad weather is forecast. If you are worried about someone’s health, contact a pharmacist, a GP or NHS 111.

Priority Services Register

If you or someone you care for is older, disabled, has a long-term health condition or relies on medical equipment, you may be able to join your energy and water companies’ free Priority Services Register for extra help in a power cut or water cut.

Help with heating costs

These are the official figures for winter 2026 to 2027, from GOV.UK, nidirect and mygov.scot. Check the links for full rules.

  • Winter Fuel Payment (England, Wales and Northern Ireland): if you were born on or before 27 June 1960, you could get between £100 and £300. Most people get it automatically. If your income is over £35,000, HMRC will take it back. See GOV.UK or nidirect.
  • Cold Weather Payment (England, Wales and Northern Ireland): £25 for each 7-day period of very cold weather (average 0°C or below) between 1 November 2026 and 31 March 2027, if you get certain benefits such as Pension Credit or some Universal Credit claims. It is paid automatically. See GOV.UK Cold Weather Payment and who can get it.
  • Scotland: there is no Winter Fuel Payment or Cold Weather Payment. Instead, Pension Age Winter Heating Payment (between £105.55 and £316.70) and Winter Heating Payment (currently £62, for people on certain benefits, whatever the weather).
  • GOV.UK also mentions the Warm Home Discount (£150 off bills if you get Pension Credit or live in a low-income household), and help from your local council.
  • Beware of scams. These payments are made automatically. Nobody will text or email asking for your bank details.

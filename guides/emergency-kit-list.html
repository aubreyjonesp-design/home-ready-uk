Emergency kit list (UK): what to keep at home

Last checked: 18 October 2026

A simple, printable list of things to keep at home for an emergency that lasts a few days, such as a power cut or a water cut. It follows GOV.UK Prepare. You do not need to buy it all at once.

Keep it all in one place that is easy to reach in the dark, and tell everyone at home where it is. GOV.UK Prepare says that if you are escaping a fire, you should not take anything with you. Just get out.

The checklist

Tick things off as you add them. Most of it you may already have.

Light, power and news
☐A torch (battery or wind-up) and spare batteries. GOV.UK says torches are safer than candles.
☐A power bank for your phone, kept charged.
☐A battery or wind-up radio for updates, with spare batteries.
☐A back-up battery for any medical equipment someone relies on, kept charged (GOV.UK Prepare).
Water and food
☐Bottled water. See how much water to store.
☐Food that does not need cooking, such as ready-to-eat tins, and a tin opener (GOV.UK Prepare).
☐Food for any pets.
Health and hygiene
☐Enough of each regular medicine to last several days, and paper copies of instructions for any medical devices (GOV.UK Prepare).
☐A first aid kit or some first aid items. GOV.UK Prepare’s list includes waterproof plasters, bandages, a thermometer, antiseptic, eyewash, sterile dressings, gloves, medical tape and tweezers. See our first aid guide.
☐Hand sanitiser and wet wipes, for when the water is off.
Papers, cash and keys
☐A paper list of important numbers: 999, 105 for a power cut (03457 643643 in Northern Ireland), family, neighbours and your GP. Your phone may not work.
☐A copy of your household emergency plan (Ready Scotland suggests keeping it in your kit).
☐Copies of important documents in a waterproof bag (British Red Cross).
☐Some cash for essentials, spare house and car keys, and a pencil and paper (British Red Cross).
Babies and pets (if needed)
☐Nappies and wipes.
☐Ready-to-feed baby formula. GOV.UK Prepare says it is best, because you may not be able to boil water or sterilise bottles.
☐Pet food, any pet medicines and a carrier.
Useful extras, if you have room
☐Blankets and warm clothes (British Red Cross).
☐A whistle (Ready Scotland).
☐Large-print or tactile information, spare glasses and hearing aid batteries, if anyone needs them (Ready Scotland).

Keep it cheap and low-waste

  • Build it up a bit at a time. GOV.UK Prepare suggests adding to your kit when you can, rather than buying everything at once.
  • Store food and drink you already use, and eat the oldest first, so nothing goes out of date.
  • Set a reminder to check torch batteries, power banks and dates twice a year. The clocks changing is an easy prompt.

A small kit for the car

GOV.UK Prepare suggests keeping a torch, an in-car phone charger, warm clothes and blankets, high-visibility clothing, jump leads, food and drink, and a snow shovel in the car boot in winter, plus a first aid kit.

If someone at home needs extra help

Ask your energy and water companies about the free Priority Services Register. GOV.UK Prepare also has advice for disabled people and carers. Our kit and water calculator adds items for medicines, medical equipment, mobility and pets.
